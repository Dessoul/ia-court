# APPLICATION TO THE COURT

**Case:** FreeBuild s.r.o. v. DanuTech d.o.o.
**Filed:** 5 March 2026
**Applicant:** FreeBuild s.r.o. (Company Reg. No. LL-2023-0312), represented by Elena Petrović, Managing Director
**Defendant:** DanuTech d.o.o. (Company Reg. No. LL-2024-0847), represented by Viktor Horváth, CEO

---

## I. Introduction

1. FreeBuild s.r.o. ("the Applicant") brings this claim against DanuTech d.o.o. ("the Defendant") for payment of sums due under a Service Agreement dated 15 August 2025 ("the Agreement"), and for damages arising from the Defendant's wrongful conduct.

2. The Applicant is a software development company registered in Liberland, specializing in blockchain infrastructure. The Defendant is a technology company registered in Liberland, operating a settlement management platform.

3. This Court has jurisdiction pursuant to Article 8.1 of the Agreement and Article 15 of the Judicial Process Law of Liberland.

---

## II. Statement of Facts

4. On 15 August 2025, the parties entered into the Agreement whereby the Applicant would develop a blockchain-based Land Registry Module ("the Module") for the Defendant's platform. The total compensation was 50,000 LLM, payable in three milestones. (Evidence: **E-001**)

5. Milestone 1 was delivered on 30 September 2025 and accepted by the Defendant on 2 October 2025. Payment of 15,000 LLM was received. (Evidence: **E-003**)

6. Milestone 2 was delivered on 15 November 2025 and accepted by the Defendant on 18 November 2025. Payment of 15,000 LLM was received. The Defendant's CTO explicitly noted minor items as non-blocking and accepted the deliverables. (Evidence: **E-003**)

7. Between October and November 2025, the Defendant repeatedly requested features outside the scope of the Agreement — specifically batch transfer processing and parcel subdivision. The Applicant consistently informed the Defendant that these features required a formal change order per Article 6 of the Agreement. No change order was ever signed. (Evidence: **E-002**)

8. The Applicant, as a goodwill gesture, agreed to include PDF certificate generation and to design the system architecture to be "batch-extensible." These concessions were clearly communicated as voluntary additions, not contractual obligations. (Evidence: **E-002**, Emails 4 and 6)

9. On 12 November 2025, the Defendant migrated its staging environment to a new infrastructure version without timely notice to the Applicant. The Applicant was notified only on 19 November 2025 — after submitting M2 deliverables. This forced the Applicant to re-do substantial integration testing work for M3. (Evidence: **E-007**)

10. Milestone 3 was delivered on 28 January 2026, six weeks after the original deadline of 15 December 2025. The delay was caused by: (a) additional work on the PDF certificate feature requested by the Defendant; (b) architectural modifications to make the system batch-extensible per the Defendant's request; and (c) the Defendant's unannounced infrastructure migration. (Evidence: **E-004**)

11. On 7 February 2026, the Defendant rejected M3, citing six deficiencies. The Applicant accepted three as legitimate (performance, authentication, documentation) and disputed three (batch transfers, subdivision, certificate verification). The Applicant offered to fix all legitimate deficiencies within the cure period. (Evidence: **E-004**, **E-005**)

12. On 14 February 2026 — only 5 business days into the 15-business-day cure period — the Defendant engaged a third-party developer (ChainForge Solutions) to modify the Applicant's source code, including building the out-of-scope features (batch transfers and subdivision). (Evidence: **E-006**)

13. The Defendant did not allow the Applicant to complete the cure period before engaging the third party.

14. The Defendant has not paid the M3 balance of 20,000 LLM. Under Article 4.2 of the Agreement, the Applicant retains all intellectual property rights in the Module until full payment.

15. A pre-filing negotiation was attempted per Article 8.2 of the Agreement, but concluded without agreement on 28 February 2026. (Evidence: **E-008**)

## III. Legal Basis

### A. The Defendant's obligation to pay arises from the Agreement and from the Constitution

16. The Agreement is a valid and binding contract between two Liberland-registered companies, governed by Liberland law (Agreement, Art. 9).

17. Under Article 5 of the Constitution (Freedom of Transaction) and Article 2 (Private Property), the obligations voluntarily assumed under the Agreement are enforceable. The Defendant's refusal to pay for work performed constitutes a deprivation of the Applicant's property (the earned compensation) without legitimate justification.

18. Under Article 6 of the Constitution (Non-Aggression Principle), the Defendant's withholding of payment for work substantially performed constitutes an act upon property the Defendant does not own — namely, the compensation earned by the Applicant.

### B. Substantial performance of the Agreement

19. The Applicant substantially performed its obligations under the Agreement. Of the six deficiencies identified by the Defendant:
   - Three were acknowledged and offered for cure (performance, authentication, documentation)
   - Two were features never included in the Agreement (batch transfers, subdivision)
   - One was disputed but offered as goodwill (certificate verification)

20. The commonly upheld principles of justice in civilized countries (per Article 39 §2(c) of the Judicial Process Law) recognize the doctrine of substantial performance: where a party has performed the essential purpose of a contract with only minor deviations, the other party cannot refuse payment entirely but may claim damages for the deviations.

### C. The Defendant breached the Agreement by preventing cure

21. Article 2.3 of the Agreement provides a 15-business-day cure period. The Defendant engaged a third party after only 5 business days, before the Applicant could complete the cure.

22. Under commonly upheld principles of justice, a party who prevents the other party from performing a contractual obligation cannot rely on that non-performance as grounds for withholding payment.

### D. The Defendant infringed the Applicant's intellectual property rights

23. Under Article 4.2 of the Agreement, the Applicant retains all IP rights until full payment. The M3 balance has not been paid.

24. By providing the source code to ChainForge and authorizing modifications, the Defendant acted upon the Applicant's property without consent, in violation of Article 6 of the Constitution (Non-Aggression Principle).

25. Article 4.3 grants a "non-exclusive license to use the Module for testing purposes during the development period." This does not authorize providing the source code to third parties or modifying it.

## IV. Relief Sought

26. The Applicant respectfully requests that the Court order:

   **(a)** Payment of the Milestone 3 balance: **20,000 LLM**

   **(b)** Late payment interest at 0.05% per calendar day from 28 January 2026 (date of M3 delivery) to the date of payment, currently amounting to approximately **360 LLM**

   **(c)** Damages for intellectual property infringement (unauthorized provision of source code to third party and unauthorized modifications): **5,000 LLM**

   **(d)** Court costs

27. **Total claim: 25,360 LLM** (plus continuing interest and court costs)
